FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

For GST inclusive calculation, the entered amount already includes GST. The tool divides the amount by (1 + GST rate/100) to find the original amount, then calculates the GST component. For example, if the inclusive amount is ₹1,180 at 18% GST, the original amount is ₹1,000 and GST is ₹180.
For GST exclusive calculation, the entered amount does not include GST. The tool multiplies the amount by the GST rate/100 to find the GST component, then adds it to get the total. For example, if the exclusive amount is ₹1,000 at 18% GST, the GST is ₹180 and total is ₹1,180.
CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST) apply to intra-state (within the same state) transactions, with GST split equally between center and state. IGST (Integrated GST) applies to inter-state (between different states) transactions, where the full GST goes to the center. For example, at 18% GST: Intra-state = 9% CGST + 9% SGST; Inter-state = 18% IGST.
India has five main GST slabs: 5% (essential items), 12% (processed food, fabrics), 18% (most goods and services), and 28% (luxury items, sin goods). A special 3% rate applies to gold and precious stones. Additionally, 0.25% applies to rough precious stones and 0.1% to uncut diamonds.
For intra-state transactions, the total GST is split equally between CGST and SGST. Each component is exactly half of the total GST amount. For example, if total GST is ₹180 at 18% rate, then CGST = ₹90 (9%) and SGST = ₹90 (9%).

Reverse GST calculation means finding the original pre-tax amount from a GST-inclusive price. The formula is: Original Amount = Inclusive Amount ÷ (1 + GST Rate/100). This is useful when you know the final price and need to determine the base price and tax component separately.
