FAQ

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a value-added tax levied on the supply of goods and services. It is used in over 160 countries worldwide under various names — GST in India, Australia, Canada, Singapore, New Zealand; VAT in UK, EU, UAE, and most other countries.
Add Tax (Exclusive): Tax = Amount × Rate ÷ 100; Total = Amount + Tax.
Remove Tax (Inclusive): Net = Total × 100 ÷ (100 + Rate); Tax = Total − Net.
Over 160 countries have GST/VAT. Major ones include India (18%), Australia (10%), Canada (5% + provincial), New Zealand (15%), Singapore (9%), UK (20%), UAE (5%), Saudi Arabia (15%), and all EU member states. The US is the only major economy without a national VAT/GST.
GST and VAT are essentially the same type of consumption tax. The naming varies by country. India, Australia, Canada, Singapore, and New Zealand call it GST. The UK, EU, and most other countries call it VAT. The calculation method is identical.

HST (Harmonized Sales Tax) is a blended tax combining Canada's federal GST (5%) with provincial sales tax. Participating provinces (ON, NB, NL, NS, PEI) charge 13-15% HST instead of separate GST+PST. Non-participating provinces charge 5% GST plus separate PST.
